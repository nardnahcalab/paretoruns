3. Key Insights

Long Output Amplifies the Gap

At 128-token, Nemotron was 5.2x faster than Qwen NVFP4. At 512-token, the gap narrows to 1.9x (275 vs 143 tok/s). This is because longer outputs shift the bottleneck from decode speed to KV cache memory, where both models face similar constraints.

Muse Nearly Fails at 512-token

Muse completes only 2 runs (c1-c2) for 512-token. At c1, it takes 63 seconds per request — 2.8x longer than Qwen NVFP4. The dense architecture can't handle long outputs efficiently on GB10's 128GB memory.

Qwen FP8 vs NVFP4: Consistent

FP8 achieves 109 tok/s, NVFP4 achieves 143 tok/s at their best configurations. NVFP4's 4-bit quantization actually produces faster decode — likely because smaller weights fit better in cache. FP8's advantage is only in precision-sensitive workloads.

Latency Dominates at 512-token

E2E latency ranges from 14.5s (Nemotron c8) to 61s (Muse c2). For user-facing applications, only Nemotron at c1-c4 provides acceptable latency (< 10s). All other configurations exceed the 5-second interactive threshold.
