Entropy Formula

H = −Σ(pi × log2(pi)) / log2(K)

Where pi is the fraction of tokens routed to expert i, and K = 256 (total experts). Normalized so that 1.0 = perfectly uniform routing across all experts, and 0.0 = all tokens routed to a single expert. With top-6 routing (2.3% of experts active per token), a perfectly balanced router would give entropy close to 1.0.

Position Analysis (Multi-Turn)

Tokens are binned by position within each output sequence:
  • Early: First 20% of output tokens — captures initial routing behavior
  • Mid: Middle 60% of output tokens — steady-state routing
  • Late: Last 20% of output tokens — routing at sequence completion
This reveals whether the router changes behavior as sequences progress, which has implications for KV cache management and load balancing in long-running inference.

1. Architecture: Hybrid Mamba-MoE

Key Finding: Nemotron 3.5 Lightning is NOT a pure MoE model. It alternates between Mamba/SSM layers (where all tokens route to expert 0 — effectively a single-path layer) and true MoE layers (where 256 experts compete with top-6 routing). This hybrid design explains the "Lightning" branding — Mamba layers provide constant-time sequence processing while MoE layers provide capacity.

6. Why Entropy Is Similar Across Datasets

Paradox: The router selects different experts (low Jaccard) but produces similar entropy (similar distribution shape). Entropy measures how evenly tokens are spread, not which experts receive tokens.

1. Training Regularization

The router is trained with load-balancing loss terms that enforce uniform token distribution across experts. This constrains entropy to a narrow band (~0.72-0.84) regardless of input content.

2. Top-6 Constraint

With only 6 of 256 experts active per token (2.3%), the router has limited freedom to vary distribution shape. The constraint forces similar entropy regardless of which experts are chosen.

3. Short Sequences

Probe sequences are short (8 decode tokens). The router may exhibit more content-dependent behavior on longer sequences where semantic context is richer.

7. Expert Parallelism

8. Research Findings

Architecture Uniqueness

Nemotron's 29/23 Mamba-MoE split is unique among production LLMs. The SSM layers handle sequence modeling at constant cost, while MoE layers provide capacity. This makes it faster than pure MoE models for long sequences.

Entropy Is Not Content-Awareness

Similar entropy (0.75-0.77) across datasets does NOT mean the router is content-agnostic. Low Jaccard (0.095 text→random) proves the router selects different experts. Entropy measures shape, not identity.

Top-6 vs Top-8

With 6 experts active per token (2.3%), Nemotron has slightly lower expert utilization than Qwen's top-8 (3.1%). This means each expert handles a larger share of tokens, potentially increasing load imbalance.

Production Implications

The content-aware routing (text vs random = 0.61 cosine) means workload composition affects load distribution. Systems serving predominantly text traffic will see different hotspots than those serving random/noisy inputs.

9. Implications for Production

Good news for deployment:
  1. Predictable load: Entropy is stable across input types (0.75-0.77). Load balancing strategies work regardless of traffic composition.
  2. Mamba speedup: 56% of layers are constant-time SSM operations. Long-sequence throughput is better than pure MoE models.
  3. Moderate imbalance: Gini 0.75-0.82 is manageable with standard load-balancing techniques.
Watch out for:
  1. EP load imbalance: With top-6 and Gini ~0.82, the hottest expert may get 3-4x average load. All-to-all communication becomes the bottleneck.
  2. Prefill routing: The single-turn probe includes prefill tokens (~65-70% of measured tokens). Real decode-only routing may differ.
  3. Content-dependent hotspots: Text and random produce different cosine patterns (0.61). Traffic mix affects which GPUs are busiest.
