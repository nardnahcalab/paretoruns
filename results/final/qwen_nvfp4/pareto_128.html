5. Analysis

Content-Agnostic Performance

All three datasets produce nearly identical performance. At c1, decode speed varies by only 3% (22.0-22.7 tok/s). At c8, the spread widens slightly to 6% (18.7-19.9 tok/s). This confirms Qwen's router is content-agnostic — input type does not affect inference speed. The minor differences are within measurement noise.

TTFT Scaling

TTFT increases 17-25% from c1 to c8 (184-232 ms). This is scheduling overhead from concurrent requests competing for GPU resources. The increase is modest — GB10 handles 8 concurrent requests with minimal prefill degradation.

ITL Degradation

ITL increases 20-30% from c1 to c8 (44-53 ms). Each additional concurrent request adds ~1.2 ms to ITL. This is the cost of sharing GPU compute across multiple decode streams.

Power & Efficiency

Peak efficiency: 4.33 tok/s/W at c8 (random). Power consumption scales from 25W (c1) to 34W (c8), a 36% increase. But throughput scales 5.3x (0.17 → 1.11 req/s). This means power efficiency improves 4.8x from c1 to c8 — batching is highly power-efficient on GB10.

6. Capacity Planning

GB10 limits: At c8, ITL reaches 53 ms (19 tok/s per stream). For 10K concurrent users, you'd need ~1,250 GB10 nodes at c8, or ~500 nodes at c32 (extrapolated from Nemotron data). The sublinear scaling (0.536 exponent) means diminishing returns beyond c8.
